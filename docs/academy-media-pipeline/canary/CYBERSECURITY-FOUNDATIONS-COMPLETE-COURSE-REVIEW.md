## Course learning architecture

### Module 1: Cybersecurity as business and mission risk

Learners connect people, processes, data, systems, trust, safety, revenue, and operations to cybersecurity decisions. The module introduces assets, threats, vulnerabilities, controls, consequences, and accountable risk ownership.

Required application:

1. Identify a business or mission process.
2. Identify the people, information, systems, and dependencies supporting it.
3. Describe the consequence of disruption or compromise.
4. Identify who owns the business decision.
5. Define the first defensible next step.

### Module 2: Recognizing and evaluating common risk indicators

Learners evaluate identity, phishing, endpoint, data, social-engineering, and account-compromise indicators without overstating incomplete evidence.

Required application:

1. Separate observations from assumptions.
2. Identify what evidence is available.
3. Identify what evidence is missing.
4. Explain potential business impact.
5. Select a proportionate reporting or escalation path.

### Module 3: Identity, controls, and decision authority

Learners distinguish preventive, detective, responsive, and recovery controls and connect multifactor authentication, least privilege, secure configuration, patching, backups, logging, and reporting to accountable decisions.

Required application:

1. Evaluate an urgent access request.
2. Identify approval and separation-of-duty requirements.
3. Determine the minimum necessary access.
4. Identify monitoring and expiration conditions.
5. Document the accountable decision owner.

### Module 4: Safe response and incident reporting

Learners practice safe pause, verification, evidence preservation, reporting, escalation, and communication when they encounter suspicious access, messages, exposed data, or unsafe requests.

Required application:

1. Identify what must be preserved.
2. Avoid unauthorized containment or investigation.
3. Select the approved reporting channel.
4. State what is known, unknown, and assumed.
5. Escalate according to role and potential consequence.

### Module 5: Secure operating habits and continuous improvement

Learners connect everyday behavior, after-action review, lessons learned, corrective action, metrics, and resilience.

Required application:

1. Identify one repeated operating habit that reduces risk.
2. Define the evidence that demonstrates the habit is working.
3. Identify the owner of corrective action.
4. Establish a review date.
5. Define how the organization will learn from failure or near misses.
